Wayfair Professional · Allstate Protection

Explain the coverage.
Then make them use it.

Eligibility, exclusions, resolutions, and the path from cart to coverage. I split that into four lessons, then gave sellers a place to try examples and check what they understood.

From brief to finished course

I turned the source material into a lesson sequence, wrote and built the course in Articulate, and designed the interactions and the quiz. My manager and subject-matter experts reviewed it for accuracy.

Practice, then feedback

Flip cards and expandable examples let people look up a specific rule. A sorting activity asks them to classify a coverage situation. A five-question quiz explains why an answer is right or wrong.

Flip cards and expandable lists keep exceptions off the main path. The sorting task is the shift from reading a rule to making a coverage decision, which is the job.

Five questions at the end. Right or wrong, the feedback names the rule, so the quiz still teaches.

How a week gets made +

More than a weekly update

One producer, from the brief to the final cut.

I research, write, host, film, design, and edit Weekly Watch. Each week I take information from around the business and turn it into a video sales teams can follow. My team, my manager, and subject-matter experts keep the facts honest.

Keep people current

Explain a business or tool change in plain language, tied to what people actually do that week.

Show up in new-hire class

Episodes get reused in onboarding, so a Friday video can keep teaching after the calendar moves on.

Short training when something changes

A few minutes on a tool, a process, or an immediate need, instead of waiting for a longer course.

What I do each week
  1. Research and shape the topic

    I bring an idea to my team, or I answer a need from my manager. I gather source material from marketing, tools, or operations, and I decide what the audience actually has to understand.

  2. Write and plan the visuals

    I turn a dense brief into a script people can hear once. I mark where a graphic or a pause will help. My team, manager, and the requestor sign off before I film.

  3. Film and make the graphics

    I deliver on camera, film, and build the visuals in Adobe tools or Canva so the spoken explanation and the picture stay together.

  4. Edit and review

    I cut in Final Cut Pro and set the pace. My team and subject-matter experts review the final for accuracy.

This episode

It introduces Sales Suite, and shows how admin tools and the storefront meet in a seller’s day. Interface examples, graphics, and a short rollout overview make the change feel real.

Why I picked it

It is a tool update turned into bite-sized training. On-camera explanation, interface examples, and custom graphics, so sellers can see what is changing, why it matters, and what to expect.

How the interviews were built +

Talking Shop

Research before the interview.
A point of view in the edit.

The series was for Wayfair Professional customers. Episodes surfaced as recommendations when professionals signed in. Marketing owned the channel. I owned the conversation and the cut.

What I owned

I researched the guests, wrote scripts and questions, hosted, edited, and made the graphics. Furniture and design knowledge I picked up on the job helped me ask better questions, not just polite ones.

How we worked

Guests’ teams reviewed scripts and questions. Another team handled recording and logistics. I handled editorial prep, hosting, and post-production, with marketing as the partner on the live site.

The better I prepare, the more room the guest has to talk.

Why these two

The family-business episode is an interview about how people actually work. The design-trends episode is a different guest and a different subject, in the same series. Together they show research, partnership, and a consistent look.

Why this belongs with the training work

Interviewing an expert and building a course ask for the same thing. Understand the subject well enough to make it clear for someone else. This time the someone else was a customer.

Produced in 2023. The 2024 date in Episode 5 refers to the trends discussed. Original episodes remain hosted on Wayfair’s website.
